# OctoAcme Project Management Overview

OctoAcme runs projects with an iterative, outcome-driven process grounded in clear roles, lightweight artifacts, and a predictable delivery rhythm. Projects begin with a one‑pager to capture the problem, objective, success metrics, stakeholders, and a high‑level timeline; teams move through initiation, planning, execution, release, and close/retrospective stages. Core principles emphasize customer-first design, incremental delivery, data‑informed decisions, and psychological safety; primary artifacts include the project charter/one‑pager, prioritized backlog and release plan, definition of done, and a standing risk register.

Operational workflows are standardized: teams use a Kanban-style project board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done), timeboxed planning to pull work that meets the Definition of Done, and a PR workflow that favors small changes (<= 400 lines when feasible), automated CI (tests, linting, and security scans), and at least one approval before merge. Backlog items include clear acceptance criteria and owners, and release planning emphasizes staging verification, rollback plans, and documented release notes.

Communication and cadence are role-focused and regular: daily standups for progress and blockers, weekly delivery syncs and PM/PdM alignment, sprint demos or milestone reviews, and monthly stakeholder updates with ad‑hoc escalation paths for urgent issues. Risks and dependencies are tracked in a risk register (ID, impact, likelihood, owner, mitigation, status), and communication templates exist for weekly status updates and incident briefings. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, and incidents produce prioritized action items that are tracked into the backlog for continuous improvement.

Quality assurance and release controls are enforced throughout the lifecycle: unit and integration tests, end‑to‑end smoke tests for critical flows, security scanning in CI, and manual QA when needed. Pre‑release requirements include passing CI and security scans, drafted release notes, and a rollback plan. Deployment follows a checklist (staging verification, automated pipeline to production where possible, post‑deploy checks), and an incident/rollback playbook is used to respond to production issues and capture remediation actions.
